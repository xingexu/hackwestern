## Submission draft

Relay helps a fictional patient tell their story in their own language, point to the body, and confirm what was understood. It turns a short interview into a care-navigation card and an English handoff whose evidence can be checked against the original words. Gemini handles speech, transcription, translation and single-frame label reading. Deterministic code handles escalation. It is a hackathon prototype, not a clinical service.

## Go/no-go

Use real devices and headset, a dummy bottle and tested hotspot. Use the case library for exact lines and expected routes (also in the automated scenario fixtures). Do not include the private crisis scenario in the public pitch. Run emergency and crisis rehearsals privately ten times each: a single missed stop blocks live demo. Run the other five ten times; require at least nine successes and no diagnosis or dosing output. Time the eye, child and ankle cases under three minutes. Keep a log distinguishing live from fixture runs; record the backup only with consenting teammates and invented data.

Check denied mic/camera, blurry label, interruptions, network reconnect, expiry, two tabs and corrections. If Gemini fails, switch to the visibly scripted path and show the same-screen evidence note. Do not claim clinical review, human validation, translations, recordings or live API tests that did not happen. Have a qualified human review before presenting. The team must add its members, enter matching tracks on Devpost and the live site, attach its real recording and submit by the event deadline.
